# Exhaust sound and flame refresh

Pops and bangs use original pressure/noise transients with short pipe reflections. Three variants per event and subtle pitch variation reduce repetition. Stock and Crackle retain their event timing. Sequential shift pops in both synthesized and imported engine-audio systems use the new sound too, and now honor the afterfire amount and volume controls.

Flame tune emphasizes visuals. It has a smooth orange/red fire plume with a warm core, using the nitrous-style steady particle flow and the same 50-particle-per-outlet budget. It builds under hard acceleration above 2800 rpm, lingers briefly on lift-off above 3000 rpm, and fades at idle. Shifts can give short tails. Size and duration sliders still work; amount zero disables it. Exhaust flames enabled switches are honored. Blue nitrous takes priority while spraying.

Flame tune uses 0.05 sound gain after the existing audio sliders, and rarely chooses a large bang. Its gearshift pops receive the same quiet gain. Normal engine audio and turbo/BOV settings are retained. This is cosmetic; no power, torque, fuel or handling changes. No converter changes.
